What the work involves

Small room, unforgiving details.

Bathrooms are the most concentrated room in a house. Plumbing supply and drain, electrical, ventilation, and a waterproof envelope all land inside about forty square feet. That density is why the work costs more per square foot than almost anywhere else in the home, and why the parts you can't see matter more than the tile you can.

Waterproofing is the whole ballgame. Tile is not waterproof and neither is grout — the membrane behind them is what keeps water out of the framing. A shower that was tiled directly over drywall will fail, and it will fail invisibly for a while first. If we open a wall and find rot, we'd rather show you a photo of it than quietly tile back over it.

Tub‑to‑shower conversions are the most common request we get, and usually the most satisfying. It's often straightforward when the drain stays put. It gets more involved when the new layout moves the drain, because that means opening the floor.

Moving fixtures also means moving vents — every drain needs one, and that's the constraint people rarely anticipate when they sketch a new layout on the back of an envelope. It's usually solvable. It's just worth knowing before you fall in love with a plan.
