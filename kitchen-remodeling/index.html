Kitchens  ·  Los Angeles & Orange County

The kitchen is usually a layout problem wearing a finish problem's clothes.

Most people call us about cabinets. Then we stand in the room and find the real issue is the wall between the kitchen and the dining room, or a peninsula that forces everyone to squeeze past the oven. We draw the plan first, and we draw it in‑house — so the person who decides where the island goes is on the same team as the person who builds it.

What the work involves

Start with how the room is used, not what it's made of.

A kitchen has three jobs happening at once — storing food, cooking it, and everyone standing around while it happens. When a kitchen feels wrong, it's usually because those three are fighting over the same floor space. That's a layout question, and it's the cheapest thing to change while the room is already open.

Opening a wall is the single change that most often transforms a house. Whether it's straightforward depends on whether the wall is carrying load. If it is, the fix is a beam and posts carrying weight down to the foundation — real structural work, engineered and inspected, not a weekend job. We'll tell you which kind of wall you have before you spend anything.

Moving plumbing and gas is the other thing that quietly drives a kitchen project. Relocating a sink means opening the floor and re-running drain lines at the right slope. Moving a range may mean a new gas line and different venting. None of it is exotic, but it's the difference between a refresh and a remodel, and it's worth knowing which one you're buying at the start.

Anything that moves a wall, a drain, or a gas line needs a permit in California, and inspections come with it. We'll walk you through which parts of your project need one when we come out to measure.
