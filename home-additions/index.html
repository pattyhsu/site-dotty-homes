Additions & ADUs  ·  Los Angeles & Orange County

More house, without moving house.

Adding square footage is the biggest thing you can do to a home short of tearing it down, and it's the project where the drawing matters most. Where the addition meets the existing house — the rooflines, the floor levels, the way you walk from old to new — is what separates an addition that reads as part of the house from one that reads as a box stuck on the back.

What the work involves

The seam between old and new is the whole design problem.

An addition is really two projects: the new structure, and the surgery on the existing house where it connects. The second one is where the surprises live. Older homes in Los Angeles and Orange County were built to codes that have changed several times since, and opening up an exterior wall is how you find out what's actually holding the place up.

Foundations, framing, and roofline all have to be resolved before anything gets pretty. A second story is a different animal again — the existing foundation and walls have to be able to carry the new load, which sometimes means reinforcement below before anything goes up above.

ADUs — accessory dwelling units, the backyard cottage or the converted garage — have become far easier to build in California than they used to be, because the state has repeatedly overridden local rules that blocked them. What's allowed on your specific lot still depends on your city, your setbacks, and your utilities, and the rules keep moving.

A garage conversion is usually the least expensive path to an ADU, because the slab and walls already exist. It also usually means giving up the garage, which is a bigger emotional decision than people expect. We'll lay out both options honestly when we come look.
